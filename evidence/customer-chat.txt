Lena: Hi, I run a small neighborhood salon. Last-minute cancellations leave gaps in our day, and I spend a lot of time texting people who asked for an earlier appointment. I’d like a simple way to handle that without all the back-and-forth.
You: Hi Lena! Could you walk me through a recent cancellation, from the slot opening until it was filled or stayed empty? Who handled it, and what made the process difficult?
Lena: A client canceled a same-day color appointment, and Carla noticed it at the front desk. She checked the sheet and texted a few people who seemed available, but replies came in at different times and it was hard to tell who had actually accepted. We didn’t refill that slot, and keeping track of the follow-up was the difficult part.
You: When a slot opens, how do you decide which waitlisted clients are a good fit and who gets asked first? What do you record about their service, stylist, availability, and how long they've been waiting?
Lena: We look for the same service, a time they can actually make, and any stylist preference they listed. If several people fit, we usually start with whoever joined the list earliest. The sheet has their name, mobile number, requested service, preferred stylist, general availability, and when they were added.
You: Would you prefer to offer the opening to one eligible client at a time or contact several together? How long should someone have to answer, and what should happen if they decline or don't reply?
Lena: I’d normally contact several because I want the opening filled quickly. Give them about 15 minutes to answer; if someone declines or doesn’t reply, move on to the next person.
You: If two people reply yes a few seconds apart, who should get the appointment, and what should the other person hear? Does a yes confirm the booking immediately, or do you want a staff member to approve it first?
Lena: The first yes should get it, and the other person should be told it was just taken. A yes should confirm it immediately; I don’t want staff approval slowing things down.
You: To make that precise: how many matching clients should we contact in the first group? If nobody accepts within 15 minutes, should we invite another group, and should a late yes from the earlier group still be allowed to take the slot?
Lena: I’d contact all the matching clients at once so we have the best chance of filling it quickly. If nobody accepts in 15 minutes, invite the next group, but a late yes from the first group should still be allowed if the slot is open.
You: I want to avoid sending confusing repeat offers. If we've already contacted everyone who matches, who would be in the next group after 15 minutes? And how close to the appointment start should we stop trying to fill it?
Lena: If we’ve contacted everyone who matches, there wouldn’t really be a next group unless someone’s availability changed. We usually stop trying when there isn’t enough time for the client to get here, but I don’t have a fixed cutoff.
You: What should a client see in an offer and a confirmation, and how would you like them to respond? Do clients tend to send a clear yes/no, ask questions, reply twice, or change their minds after accepting?
Lena: The offer should show the service, stylist, date, time, and how long they have to respond, with clear accept and decline choices. Confirmation should repeat those details and say the appointment is theirs. Clients usually give a clear yes or no, though sometimes they change their minds after accepting.
You: If the client who accepted then cancels, what should happen next? Also, what should staff be able to do if the slot is filled by a phone call, the stylist becomes unavailable, or they need to stop the outreach?
Lena: If the accepting client cancels, we should offer the slot to the next eligible person. Staff should be able to mark it filled, cancel the opening, or stop outreach if the stylist becomes unavailable. There should also be a clear way to see and end any active offer.
You: What failures worry you most—for example, a text not sending, a client replying after the slot is taken, or the computer closing midway through outreach? What should staff see or be able to do when one of those happens?
Lena: A client replying after it’s taken is a big concern, and messages can also fail to send. Staff should see the current status and any failed or late responses, and be able to close the opening or contact someone manually. I don’t know what the computer closing midway would require technically.
You: You don't need to choose anything technical. From the salon's perspective, if Carla closes the page and another colleague opens it later, should the existing offers keep going with their original timing and replies visible? For a failed text, would you want another attempt, or staff to handle it immediately?
Lena: Yes, existing offers should keep going with their original timing, and replies should still be visible when someone reopens it. For a failed text, staff should see it immediately and handle that client manually rather than retrying automatically.
You: Who would use the staff screen day to day, and what must they be able to see at a glance for each opening? Which actions would save the most time—adding clients to the waitlist, opening an offer, checking replies, or something else?
Lena: Lena and Carla would use it. For each opening, we need to see the current offer holder, who declined or timed out, the remaining candidates, and the final result. Opening the offer and checking replies are the biggest time savers.
You: When someone takes an earlier appointment, do they already have a later booking that staff must move or cancel? And if two openings overlap, should the same client be allowed to accept both, or should accepting one remove them from the other offers?
Lena: Some clients may already have a later booking, but staff would handle moving or canceling that in Square. A client shouldn’t be able to accept overlapping openings; accepting one should remove them from the other active offers.
You: Could you give me a realistic example opening—service, stylist, date/time, and duration—and two or three waitlist entries that should or shouldn't match it? Also, if no one matches or everyone says no, what outcome should staff see?
Lena: For example, a 60-minute haircut with Maya this Saturday at 2 p.m. would match someone wanting a haircut who can come then and is open to Maya. Someone wanting color or only a different stylist would not match. If nobody matches or everyone declines, staff should see that the opening stayed unfilled.
You: You mentioned a 15-minute response window but allowing late acceptances while the slot is free. After 15 minutes, should we simply flag nonresponders for staff and keep the opening available, rather than closing it? Would it help for staff to choose a 'stop accepting at' time for each opening based on travel time?
Lena: Yes, keep the opening available and flag nonresponders for staff. A staff-set “stop accepting at” time would be helpful, since travel time varies.
You: What would make you say this was successful after a couple of weeks—more openings filled, less staff time texting, fewer booking mistakes, or something else? Roughly how many cancellations and waitlisted clients do you typically handle, and which result matters most?
Lena: Success would mean filling more openings without staff repeatedly checking or texting. We handle roughly 8 to 12 last-minute cancellations a week, with around 20 to 30 people on the waitlist. If I had to pick one, reliable movement to the next person without double-booking matters most.
You: I want to make sure the prototype follows the right approach. You preferred contacting everyone together, and you also emphasized moving reliably to the next person. Which should we demonstrate: (A) invite all matching clients at once, with the first yes winning, or (B) offer to the earliest person first and move to the next after a decline or 15 minutes? Both would prevent double-booking.
Lena: B is safer. I don’t want another Saturday situation. One at a time is probably safer if it keeps moving on its own.
You: Under that one-at-a-time approach, suppose Alex's 15 minutes expire and the offer moves to Sam. Alex then replies yes while Sam is still deciding. Should Alex be told their offer expired so Sam keeps their chance, even though the slot isn't booked yet?
Lena: Yes. Alex should be told the offer expired, and Sam should keep the chance to accept while the slot is still open.
You: If the current client's offer fails to send, you wanted staff alerted and no automatic retries. Should the opening move immediately to the next eligible client, or pause until staff decides what to do? What should the dashboard show for the failed client?
Lena: It should pause until staff decides. The dashboard should show that the offer failed, when it failed, and give staff the option to contact that client manually or move to the next person.
You: If someone declines this particular opening, should they stay on the waitlist for future openings? If they accept and later cancel, should we resume with the next eligible person rather than offer it back to them?
Lena: A decline should leave them on the waitlist for future openings. If they accept and later cancel, resume with the next eligible person rather than offering it back to them.
You: For the first demo, I'll use sample clients and simulated messages so we can safely show acceptance, declines, timeouts, a failed send, and staff taking over. Actual texting and changes in Square would remain outside the demo. Which scenario would most convince you this solves the problem before trying it in the salon?
Lena: Show a same-day cancellation where the first client times out, the next client accepts, and the offer is filled without double-booking. I’d also want to see a failed message pause the process and make the handoff to staff clear.
You: Here's my understanding: match the service, time availability, and stylist preference, then contact the earliest waitlisted eligible client one at a time. Give each offer 15 minutes; a decline or timeout moves to the next person, and expired offers cannot claim the slot. The current client's yes confirms immediately and ends their other active offers. Failed sends pause for staff, with no automatic retry. Lena and Carla can see the current client, history, remaining candidates and final outcome, and can move on, mark filled, or stop an opening. Closing the page must not reset the process. Staff set the final cutoff; exhausting the list means unfilled. Square changes stay manual. Is that accurate, and is there any essential correction?
Lena: Yes, that’s accurate. The essential point is that a client who accepts and later cancels should not be offered the same opening again; continue with the next eligible person.
You: One follow-up on the staff handoff: if an offer fails and Carla calls the client, should she be able to record an acceptance or decline directly in this screen? If the client doesn't answer the phone, should Carla explicitly choose to move on, rather than the process guessing what happened?
Lena: Yes, Carla should be able to record an acceptance or decline after calling. If the client doesn’t answer, she should explicitly choose to move on; the process shouldn’t guess.
You: When a client accepts one opening, should their other active offers all end—even ones on another day—or only offers that overlap in time? And should those other openings immediately continue to their next eligible client?
Lena: Only overlapping offers should end. Those openings should immediately continue to their next eligible client.
You: Last timing detail: if staff set 'stop accepting at' to 1:30 and the next offer goes out at 1:25, should that client get only the remaining five minutes? Should the opening also close at that cutoff if a failed message is still waiting for staff to handle it?
Lena: Yes, the client should get only the remaining five minutes. The opening should close at 1:30 even if a failed message is still waiting for staff.
You: If a confirmed client cancels and we continue filling that same opening, should we keep the original queue order and skip everyone who already declined, timed out, or canceled, rather than starting the list over? Would you want staff to explicitly reopen it before any new offer is sent?
Lena: Keep the original queue order and skip anyone who already declined, timed out, or canceled. Staff should explicitly reopen it before another offer is sent.
You: If staff cancel an opening or mark it filled by phone while someone still has an active offer, what should that client be told? Should their offer immediately become unavailable, and should the staff history distinguish 'filled by staff' from 'canceled' and 'unfilled'?
Lena: Their offer should immediately become unavailable, and they should be told the opening is no longer available. The history should distinguish filled by staff, canceled, and unfilled.
